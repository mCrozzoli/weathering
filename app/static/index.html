How it connects

Start

  1. Install the boards once. Open Boards, plug each ESP32 in by USB and install its part: Sensor box, Hub, Sculpture. Needs Chrome or Edge.
  2. Calibrate the sculpture once. With its ESP32 on USB and the motors on 12 V, follow Boards → Sculpture motors: find the motors, set slack with the structure open, then set each string's closed point.
  3. Power the sensor box and the sculpture. They find the hub by themselves (no addresses to type).
  4. Plug the hub into the computer and double-click Weathering.command. This page opens and connects. Three dots in the top bar turn green: hub, sensor, sculpture.
  5. Sound starts with it (SuperCollider). Without a sound card it plays four virtual panels; Touch knocks on one. With the four piezos and exciters on a 4-in/4-out sound card, start Weathering.command --sound-live.
  6. Leave it. A reading arrives every 5 minutes and is saved at once. It starts closed. At 06:00 and 18:00 the data is read again from the start and the strings let the sculpture open to its new shape (⚙ → Moves: live makes it also follow each reading).

If something stops

  1. Page closed: readings keep being saved by the app. Open http://127.0.0.1:8770 again, or double-click Weathering.command.
  2. Computer off or crashed: double-click Weathering.command. The show reopens where it was; a run that was missed happens at once. The sculpture kept its shape meanwhile, and the sensor box kept about a day of readings and sends them now.
  3. For weeks unattended (Mac mini): System Settings → Energy → Start up automatically after a power failure; add Weathering.command to Login Items; turn off sleep.

No hardware yet? Click Connect → Demo: a pretend room and sculpture, as fast as you like. Everything above works the same.

Boards

Plug one ESP32 in by USB, then click Connect a board. Install its part, change its settings, test it. One board at a time.

Setting up boards needs Chrome or Edge on a computer.

The app is holding the hub's USB port. To set up the hub itself, click Connect → Disconnect first.

Readings of the data

  

  
MLP
Your own mapping, learned from examples. Pick a moment on the history slider, open Teach…, shape the sculpture with the four sliders (one per string) and click Teach this moment. After two or more, a small neural network (an MLP, learning by example as in Wekinator) gives a shape for every moment, taught or not. Slide through the history to preview it before letting it move the sculpture.

  
Every 12 hours each reading looks at everything gathered since the opening and gives four numbers, one per string: how far to let it out. The highlighted one moves the sculpture, scaled by its openness: closed at first, opening as the show lives.
